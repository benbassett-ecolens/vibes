<summary>What the terms mean (click to expand)</summary>

- **P/E ratio** — the share price divided by a year of per-share profit. A P/E of 30 means you pay $30 for every $1 of annual profit. High can mean 'expensive', or it can mean 'profits are temporarily depressed'.
- **Dividend yield** — the annual cash payout as a percentage of the share price.
- **Payout ratio** — how much of the company's profit goes out as dividends. Over 100% means it is paying out more than it earns, which is not sustainable.
- **Stop** — the price at which you admit you were wrong and sell. Deciding this *before* you buy is the single most useful habit in trading.
- **Risking $1 to make $X** — the reward-to-risk ratio. Below about 2 the desk will not bother, because you have to be right too often to come out ahead.
- **Risk budget / heat** — the total you would lose if every position hit its stop at once. The desk keeps this under 6% of the account.
- **Regime** — whether the overall market is calm and rising (take more risk) or falling and jumpy (take less).
